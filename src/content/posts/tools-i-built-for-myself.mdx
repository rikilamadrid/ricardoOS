<!-- locale:en -->

I have four packages on npm. This week I looked at their download counters and couldn't stop smiling.

| Package | Weekly downloads |
| --- | --- |
| [`wonder-wagon-ui`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/wonder-wagon-ui) | 681 |
| [`create-lorekeeper`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/create-lorekeeper) | 680 |
| [`create-pathfinder`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/create-pathfinder) | 262 |
| [`forge-local-ai-kit`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/forge-local-ai-kit) | 208 |
| **Total** | **1,831** |

None of them started as a product.

## They were for me

**Lorekeeper** started with a note I couldn't find. I knew I had written the answer down somewhere in my Markdown, and I wanted a command that would hand me the passage. Offline, no API key.

**Pathfinder** is the workflow kit I use to build with AI agents without handing them the keys. It does the repetitive parts of delivery and leaves the decisions with me.

**Forge** is the plumbing I pulled out of LAMA, the old Mac I turned into an AI server, once I noticed every app I wrote was going to need the same timeouts and errors to talk to a local model.

**Wonder Wagon UI** is the design system that makes the other three look like they belong to the same family. It's the least practical of the four, and it's the most downloaded. I don't understand that, and I like it.

It's also exactly one download ahead of Lorekeeper. Neither depends on the other. I have no theory. I just keep checking.

## What the numbers are not

1,831 is not 1,831 people.

npm counts downloads, not humans. A CI pipeline that installs a package on every run counts every time. So do registry mirrors, so do bots, and so do my own machines. The two `create-` packages are installers you run with `npx`, so one curious person trying it twice is two downloads.

I don't know how many of these are real developers, and I'm not going to pretend I do.

## What they are

What I do know is roughly how often I install my own packages, and it isn't 1,831 times a week.

So the rest is somebody else. Some of it is surely a robot. Some of it, I'd like to think, is a person who typed `npx create-lorekeeper` and pointed it at their own notes, or tried Pathfinder on a real repository just to see what it would do.

I'll never know which. That's fine. The thing that gets me is simpler: **these tools left my laptop.** They're running, or at least being unpacked, on machines I'll never see, for reasons I'll never hear about.

Building for yourself is a closed loop. You have the problem, you write the fix, you're the only user, and the only feedback is whether it works for you. A download counter cracks that loop open just a little. It's a conversation where I only hear one side, and the side I hear is a number going up.

## Small number, big feeling

1,831 is not a big internet number. Plenty of packages do that before lunch.

But I made these. I named the commands, picked the colours, wrote the docs, broke a release pipeline and fixed it. Every one of those downloads lands on something I sat down and built because I wanted it to exist.

From where I'm sitting, it's enormous.

Desde donde yo lo veo, es enorme.
